# Research notes — Resonance Lab

Status: written 2026-10-01 by the research workstream. Every claim about a source below was checked
against the page or document fetched on that date. Where we could read only an abstract, we say so and
make no claim beyond it. Nothing here reports a result of Resonance Lab itself; see
`docs/EXPERIMENT_REPORT.md` for measured results.

## What Resonance Lab is and is not

- **It is** an engineered two-agent simulation. One agent (the sender) knows which of a small set of
  timing patterns is the target. It plays a short structured musical phrase. The other agent (the
  receiver) must pick the pattern from what it observes. Both agents keep a bounded state vector
  (`activation`, `expected_value`, `uncertainty`, `affiliation`), an episodic memory and a learner. The
  state can be coupled to exploration temperature, expressive tempo/velocity and learning rate.
- **State variables are operational definitions.** "Activation" means the number defined in
  `schemas.StateVector`, driven by the update equations in the code. It does not mean arousal in a
  person, and nothing in the project measures or claims experience, feeling or sentience.
- **Our question is narrow.** Can short phrases carry task information? Can they shape a persistent,
  history-dependent internal state that measurably changes later behaviour? We answer that with
  ablations (`no_history`, `state_fixed`, `state_decoupled`, `symbol`, `perturbed`), not with
  interpretation.
- **We do not claim novelty.** We have not established that this combination is new, and we do not
  claim to be first. We checked one adjacent line of work, described below. We did not survey the
  wider literatures on emergent communication, affective computing, computational musicology or
  multi-agent music systems.
  - *Adjacent line we verified:* Lazaridou, Peysakhovich & Baroni, "Multi-Agent Cooperation and the
    Emergence of (Natural) Language", arXiv:1612.07182 (abstract page; comment field: "Accepted at
    ICLR 2017"). In their referential game, a sender is told which of two images is the target and
    sends a message from a fixed, arbitrary vocabulary, and the receiver must identify the target. Our
    sender/receiver protocol has the same shape. It differs in that the messages are structured
    musical phrases with graded acoustic similarity, rather than arbitrary symbols. Our `symbol`
    condition is the arbitrary-symbol control. We read only the abstract.
- **Model-generated text is labelled as generated.** The optional model layer writes events of type
  `generated_narrative` labelled "generated interpretation — not a measurement". Those events are
  never fed into state, learning or metrics.

## Sources

### 1. Kirke & Miranda (2015), JASSS

**Citation (as on the page):** Alexis Kirke and Eduardo Miranda (2015). "A Multi-Agent Emotional
Society Whose Melodies Represent its Emergent Social Hierarchy and Are Generated by Agent
Communications." *Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation* 18(2) 16.
DOI 10.18564/jasss.2679. Received 29-Nov-2013, accepted 26-Oct-2014, published 31-Mar-2015.
Full text read at https://www.jasss.org/18/2/16.html.

**What it does (verified):** Agents have a valence/arousal state and perform monophonic MIDI tunes,
transformed by that state (inter-onset interval, duration, loudness, pitch, major/minor key). A listener
estimates the affect of what it hears with a linear model over mean pitch, loudness, key mode and
inter-onset interval. The page reports that the model was fitted on 1920 random MIDI files, with about
10%/9% average error. The listener then moves its own state toward that estimate
(`valence'_B = (1 − γ_v)·valence_B + γ_v·valenceEst_A`, and likewise for arousal). If the affect is
close enough to its own, it may append the tune to its own growing tune. An "interaction coefficient"
(`IC(A,B) += d·N`) decides who performs to whom; the authors report that this yields a social hierarchy
reflected in the final tunes. Experiments use 8 agents (plus a 2-agent case). A 10-listener study
reports that when at least 6 of the 8 agents shared an initial valence/arousal, listeners detected that
valence in the final tune 71% of the time and that arousal 82% of the time. The purpose is algorithmic
composition, with no cooperative task. The page says nothing about code availability or licence.

**What we borrow:** state pushed by features of heard music, with inertia (their γ update corresponds
to our `inertia`/drive split); expressive tempo and loudness modulated by an activation-like variable;
listener features computed from the note list, not from audio.

**What we do not implement or claim:** no valence computed from music (our `expected_value` comes
from task outcomes); no fitted affect estimator (our acoustic→activation drive is hand-authored and
switchable); no tune accumulation or social hierarchy (two agents and an `affiliation` scalar); no
listener study, and no claim about what listeners would perceive.

### 2. Novelli & Proksch (2022), Frontiers in Neurorobotics

**Citation (as on the page):** Novelli, N. and Proksch, S. (2022) Am I (Deep) Blue? Music-Making AI
and Emotional Awareness. *Front. Neurorobot.* 16:897110. doi: 10.3389/fnbot.2022.897110. Labelled a
PERSPECTIVE article; received 15 March 2022, accepted 20 May 2022, published 21 June 2022; CC BY.
Full text read.

**What it does (verified):** This is a perspective/review, not an empirical study. It reviews
music-generating systems: Magenta, BachBot and Flow Composer, then AI Duet and Cybraphon under
"Minimally Interoceptive Artificial Music Generation", then the Shimon marimba robot, then Holly
Herndon's "Spawn". It argues that these systems' limits are "rooted in current AIs lack of thoroughly
embodied, interoceptive processes associated with the emotional component of music perception and
production". The authors propose that emotional experience in music relies on "embodied expectancy of
the internal, physiological state", and they frame this in predictive/inferential terms. They conclude
that a successful music-making AI needs both generative capacity and "thoroughly embodied,
interoceptive processes". On Cybraphon, they note that its "emotional state" selects among
precomposed music, and that this state is not the product of a homeostatic body but of monitoring its
own popularity online.

**What we borrow:** the paper's separation between generating or selecting music and the internal
processes that would ground emotion, used as a vocabulary guard: our agents generate and modulate
phrases and have an engineered state, nothing more. We also take the Cybraphon example as a caution:
a state variable that selects music is not, by itself, an emotion.

**What we do not implement or claim:** No interoceptive or homeostatic body model, no predictive model
of internal physiology, and nothing that would meet the paper's bar for emotional awareness.

### 3. Bach, "A Framework for Emergent Emotions, Based on Motivation and Cognitive Modulators"

**Citation (as in the document):** Joscha Bach, "A Framework for Emergent Emotions, Based on Motivation
and Cognitive Modulators", Berlin School of Mind and Brain, Humboldt University of Berlin. Preprint PDF
at https://www.goertzel.org/BachEmotionPreprint.pdf (24 pages). The first page gives no journal, year
or volume. The PDF's embedded metadata title reads "Microsoft Word - Draft Submission Bach IJSE 2011
Formatted.doc". We
report that metadata only, and do not infer from it where or whether the paper was published. Full
text read after local PDF text extraction. WebFetch could not decode the PDF directly.

**What it does (verified):** A conceptual paper. It separates demands/urges (motivation), modulators,
affective states (regions of modulator space) and directed higher-level emotions ("Motivation
determines what is to be done, emotion shapes how it is being done"). It treats emotions as
"perceptual gestalts", not architectural modules. MicroPsi's modulators, following Dörner's Psi theory,
are: valence; arousal (urgency of active demands); resolution level (width/depth of activation
spreading, inversely related to arousal); selection threshold (an adaptive "stubbornness" bonus on the
current goal); goal directedness (lowered by uncertainty); and securing rate (background checks, raised
by uncertainty). The paper sketches Big-Five personality modelling via per-demand weight, gain, loss and
decay. It calls the framework qualitative, says MicroPsi agents have run in virtual environments and on
robots, and reports no quantitative evaluation.

**What we borrow:** the stance that state variables modulate cognition and action and are not
themselves emotions. Our coupling follows it: `uncertainty` and `activation` change softmax
temperature (compare goal directedness and resolution level), `activation` changes expressive tempo
and velocity, and `affiliation` scales the learning rate. Also per-dimension decay, and per-agent
`sensitivity` as a trait-like parameter.

**What we do not implement or claim:** No demand/urge system, no MicroPsi, no resolution level, no
selection threshold, no securing rate, and no personality model. Our four dimensions are a much smaller
engineered set, and we do not claim that emotions emerge from them.

### 4. MuseAgent-1 (2026), arXiv

**Citation (as on the page):** Qihao Zhao, Yunqi Cao, Yangyu Huang, Hui Yi Leong, Fan Zhang, Kim-Hui
Yap, Wei Hu. "MuseAgent-1: Interactive Grounded Multimodal Understanding of Music Scores and
Performance Audio." arXiv:2601.11968 (v1, 17 Jan 2026), cs.MM. Comment: "Tech Report".
**Abstract only.**

**What it does (from the abstract only):** It introduces MuseAgent, a music-centric multimodal agent.
The agent augments language models with structured symbolic representations derived from sheet-music
images (optical music recognition) and performance audio (automatic music transcription). It also
introduces MuseBench, covering music-theory reasoning, score interpretation and performance-level
analysis across text, image and audio. The abstract reports that existing multimodal LLMs perform
poorly on these tasks and that MuseAgent achieves "substantial improvements". The abstract page lists
no code or data link.

**What we borrow:** The principle that a language model reasons better over an explicit symbolic
representation of music than over raw signals. Our optional model layer gives models a *symbolic
feature summary computed from the note list*, and the prompts say so truthfully.

**What we do not implement or claim:** No OMR, no transcription, no audio input to any model, no
benchmark, and no claim about MuseAgent's numbers. We did not read the paper body.

### 5. LARA-Gen (2025), arXiv

**Citation (as on the page):** Jiahao Mei, Xuenan Xu, Zeyu Xie, Zihao Zheng, Ye Tao, Yue Ding,
Mengyue Wu. "LARA-Gen: Enabling Continuous Emotion Control for Music Generation Models via Latent
Affective Representation Alignment." arXiv:2510.05875 (v1 7 Oct 2025; v2 10 Mar 2026), cs.SD.
Comment: "Submitted to Interspeech 2026". **Abstract only.**

**What it does (from the abstract only):** It is a text-to-music framework for continuous emotion
control. It aligns the generator's internal hidden states with an external music-understanding model
(Latent Affective Representation Alignment). It adds an emotion-control module over a continuous
valence-arousal space that is disentangled from the text prompt. It also contributes a benchmark with
a curated test set and an Emotion Predictor. The abstract claims better emotion adherence and music
quality than baselines. It links a demo page of generated samples. We saw no code link.

**What we borrow:** Only the general idea that a continuous control variable can parameterise
expressive generation. Our version is a deterministic linear map from `activation` to tempo
multiplier and velocity offset.

**What we do not implement or claim:** No neural music generation, no latent alignment, no
valence-arousal conditioning and no emotion predictor. We make no claim that our phrases express or
evoke any emotion.

### 6. AffectAgent (2026), arXiv

**Citation (as on the page):** Zeheng Wang, Zitong Yu, Yijie Zhu, Bo Zhao, Haochen Liang, Taorui Wang,
Wei Xia, Jiayu Zhang, Zhishu Liu, Hui Ma, Fei Ma, Qi Tian. "AffectAgent: Collaborative Multi-Agent
Reasoning for Retrieval-Augmented Multimodal Emotion Recognition." arXiv:2604.12735 (v1 14 Apr 2026;
v2 5 Aug 2026), cs.CV. Comment: "Accepted by ACM MM 2026". **Abstract only.**

**What it does (from the abstract only):** It is a multi-agent retrieval-augmented framework for
multimodal emotion *recognition*. Three agents (query planner, evidence filter, emotion generator) are
trained jointly with MAPPO using a shared affective reward. It adds Modality-Balancing Mixture of
Experts and Retrieval-Augmented Adaptive Fusion for missing modalities. The abstract reports superior
performance on MER-UniBench. It says code "will be released" at a link. We did not check whether any
code exists.

**What we borrow:** Two loose analogues. First, a shared reward drives cooperating agents; our two
agents share the coordination score. Second, retrieval of similar past episodes informs a decision; our
episodic memory retrieves the top-k similar phrases.

**What we do not implement or claim:** No emotion recognition from human data, no MAPPO, no MoE and no
fusion. Our agents recognise nothing about human emotion.

## Confounds we expect

1. **Expressive coupling changes the signal.** When coupling is on, `activation` scales the sender's
   tempo and velocity. That moves `note_density`, `tempo` and `mean_velocity` in the receiver's
   features, so the same motif can look different depending on the sender's state. If coupling lowers
   coordination scores, that is *degraded communication*, not an affective effect. The
   `state_decoupled` condition and the `tempo_shift`/`velocity_flatten` perturbations are the controls.
2. **The symbol control may be the easier decoding problem.** The `symbol` channel passes the exact
   identity of the sender's motif as a one-hot. The receiver learns a lookup with no generation noise
   and no overlap between motifs. If symbol beats music, that need not mean music is a worse channel
   in principle. It may only mean that exact identity is easier to decode than graded, noisy features.
   It also removes acoustic drive, so it changes two things at once.
3. **The acoustic→activation drive is hand-authored.** "Dense/loud phrases raise activation" is a rule
   we wrote, not something learned. Any finding that "music changed state" through this path is a
   consequence of that rule. The `acoustic_activation_enabled` switch must accompany any such claim.
4. **History effects can be built by the schedule.** Presets such as "same phrase, different history"
   produce different states by design, through different training sequences. A difference there shows
   that the mechanism carries history forward. It is not a discovered phenomenon. The preset
   descriptions must say so (`constructed_note`).
5. **Feature normalisation and feature choice decide what "similar" means.** Generalisation to
   transposed or perturbed motifs depends on which features we compute and how we scale them, for
   example the `dissonance_proxy` definition and the interval-class bins.
6. **Runs are the unit of analysis.** Turns within a run are correlated. Condition comparisons must
   use per-seed run metrics, not pooled turns.
7. **Model-assisted decisions are not the simulation's results.** The local Qwen2.5-0.5B model is a
   weak decision-maker. Its choices are not deterministic across builds or hardware, and its
   rationales are generated text. Model-assisted runs are reported separately and never mixed into
   local-policy results.
